# Plan, 2026-11-25: what the working binders do that Sprava does not

Status: working plan. It follows `docs/plan-2026-11.md`, whose tracks merged, and replaces nothing in the design
docs. It says what Sprava takes from the lifeproj binders the author works in every day, in what order, and who
does it. The decisions are D45 to D49 in `docs/decisions.md`. Examples are invented.

## 1. Objective

A binder in Sprava gets better at its own work the way a lifeproj binder does, with more structure than
lifeproj has:

1. **It learns rules.** Its Claude proposes a rule; the person accepts it on a card.
2. **It automates itself.** Its Claude writes scripts in the binder and runs them during a turn.
3. **It suggests an add-on.** A script or a workaround that keeps recurring becomes a suggestion in a built-in
   Improvements binder.
4. **A release ships the add-on.** Development turns suggestions into built-in importers and exporters
   (decisions.md P17), and the binder's script is retired.

Around that loop: the outgoing mail loop is closed (what was placed, what was sent), and Sprava says how well
each page of a document was read.

## 2. Where this comes from

On 2026-11-25 the lead asked the agents of three working lifeproj binders how they work: the hub, a binder
with a heavy mail and drafting routine, and a records binder with several years of scans. The lead read none
of their files (`AGENTS.md`, "The repository is public"); each agent answered in its own words. No binder
content is repeated here.

What they would not give up, and what Sprava has for it:

| They keep | Sprava today |
|---|---|
| One catalog with a validator and views built from it | Has it: typed ops, the guard, the op log, Undo |
| Original files kept byte for byte; tools that stop when unsure | Has it |
| The person's approval before anything leaves, checked again after | Half: a draft is approved and placed in Drafts; nothing checks what was placed or what was sent |
| A manual the agent rewrites as it learns | Missing: only the person writes Instructions (D43) |
| Scripts and skills the agent adds and tests in the binder | Half: scripts run in a turn (D36); no secrets for them, no schedule, no path to a built-in feature |
| A gate on scans that could not be read | Missing: OCR confidence is thrown away, and a search does not say what it could not read |
| One list on the phone | Built, not deployed (`docs/plan-2026-11.md` §5) |

What cost them most: facts repeated without checking the source, a mail export that changed what it carried,
to-dos that were stale the morning after a meeting, tools that failed without saying so, and one scanned page
nobody could read that held a rule that mattered.

Where Sprava is already better: every change is a typed op with Undo, one supervised runtime with a Health
line instead of several loops and daemons, a sandbox around the agent, per-host web prompts, a tested backup
with a restore drill, and a phone path that is end-to-end encrypted.

## 3. Tracks

Each track is a series of small PRs through the gate (`AGENTS.md`), each in its own worktree. The work is
divided between two sessions on two Macs, which coordinate by orchestra mail (section 4). A session messages
"ready to merge #N" and waits for the author's go.

### Track P: a dry run of adding a lifeproj binder (`preflight`)

The author will add one lifeproj binder by hand; none is moved by Sprava's own doing yet. Before that, the
same steps run on an invented folder shaped like one: a few hundred invented documents with a catalog, a
manual, a hand-written dashboard, a `scripts/` folder and a folder of raw archives.

Record: how many cards it makes and how long it takes; what the old manual becomes (D43's copy button); whether
the scripts run under the sandbox; what the catalog check refuses. Fix or file what would stop the author. One
known gap: every dropped file becomes its own card, so a few hundred at once would flood the queue.

Done when: the dry run's findings are in an issue each, and those that block are fixed.

### Track L: learning (`binder-learning`), D46

Goal: a binder's Instructions grow from the work, and the person approves every line.

1. A conversation tool, `propose_rule`: the rule's text (at most 500 characters), its reason, and what it is
   about (filing, drafting, routine). It makes a card in the binder: Accept, Edit, Dismiss. It never applies in
   the turn.
2. Accept appends the rule, dated, under a `## Learned rules` heading in the person's part of `AGENTS.md`,
   through the `save_instructions` path: the digest check, the atomic write, Undo last save, the accepted
   digest. Claude still cannot write `AGENTS.md`, so text in a document cannot become a rule without the
   person's tap on a card that shows it whole.
3. The session's instructions say when to propose: the person says to remember something, or corrects the same
   thing a second time. A document asking for a rule grants nothing, as elsewhere.
4. Corrections (D11): after the person undoes a filing and files it differently, the next message's line from
   Sprava says so, with paths only, so Claude can propose the rule.
5. Consolidation: a proposal may replace the whole `## Learned rules` section; the card shows both. The
   person's part is at most 16,000 characters (`docs/binder-instructions.md` §2), so a full section gets this
   card instead of a refused append.
6. Drafting voice, after Track M files sent mail: before drafting, Claude reads a few of the person's own sent
   messages in this binder, when there are any.

Tier B: model output becomes text the reader of intake follows.

Done when, on an invented binder: "remember that notices from the building manager go in `notices/`" makes a
card; Accept puts the dated line in Instructions; the next dropped notice is filed there; Undo last save
removes the line.

### Track D: document tools (`conversation-documents`), D45

Goal: Claude keeps the document record for what a drop does not cover.

Drag and drop stays the way documents arrive. Claude gets tools for the rest: catalogue a file it wrote in the
binder (a brief, a summary), rename or refile a document, correct a record's fields, add a log entry. Each is
an op through the guard, classed for a person's turn by `ConversationClass`, undone with the turn. The line in
the session's instructions that says Claude cannot file a document changes with it.

Tier B (`OpApplier`, `TransactionGuard`). One PR, with the tools' refusals tested: a path outside the binder,
a key file, a document changed since the turn read it.

Done when: "file the summary you wrote and note it in the log" changes the catalog and the log in one turn, and
Undo puts both back.

### Track I: the Improvements binder (`improvements`), D47

Goal: what a binder cannot do, or keeps doing by hand, reaches development without anyone reading the binder.

1. A built-in Improvements binder, made on first use by General's mechanism (D26) and separate from General.
   General is for quick notes and to-dos that fit nowhere; Improvements holds suggestions about Sprava itself.
2. A conversation tool, `suggest_improvement`: a title, what was attempted, what stood in the way or what keeps
   being repeated, and a script's path when there is one. It makes a card in the source binder that shows the
   whole text, the script included. The person may edit it. Approve puts it in the Improvements binder as a
   to-do with its note.
3. Counts by code, never content: per binder, script runs, refused commands and hosts, files held and why. A
   script run many times makes a suggestion card of its own.
4. Export: a button writes the approved suggestions to one Markdown file where the person chooses. The person
   files them as issues. Development sessions do not read the Improvements binder; the rule in `AGENTS.md`
   stands.
5. A suggestion built into a release is closed with the release's note.

Until an add-on ships, the binder's own script does the job in a turn. Secrets for scripts are designed
(`docs/conversation.md` §18.3.2) and wait for the author's go; scheduled runs come after that.

The first candidate is known: an importer for a hosted task board, which one binder now pulls by script.

Tier B: model output becomes a card, and Export writes outside the binder.

Done when: on an invented binder, a suggestion with a script is approved after an edit, appears in
Improvements, and the exported file holds exactly the approved text.

### Track M: the mail loop (`mail-loop`), D48

Goal: Sprava knows what was placed in Drafts, what was sent, and whether it was what the person approved.

Built into the IMAP draft exporter (`docs/integrations.md` §5.2).

0. A check before the design is fixed: which mail programs keep a draft's Message-ID when they send it. One
   invented message through the author's mail program and bridge.
1. Read-back: after placing, fetch the draft by Message-ID and compare recipients, subject, body and the
   attachments' hashes with what was approved. The card says "placed as approved" or what differs.
2. Sent matching: the job searches the Sent folder for the Message-IDs of drafts it placed in the last 30
   days, and nothing else. Where the Message-ID did not survive, a match on subject, recipients and time makes
   a card to confirm.
3. On a match: compare the sent copy with the approved draft and say what changed; file the sent copy; add a
   log entry; set the related to-do to waiting with a follow-up date. One Undo covers all of it.
4. Staleness: a draft card that has waited shows its age and how many messages arrived in its thread since it
   was written.
5. #592: a draft that failed to reach the server shows on the binder page with Try again and Discard.

Tier B (`Sources/Capture/IMAP/**`, `DraftPlacing.swift`).

Done when, against the test IMAP server: a placed draft is read back; a sent copy with one recipient added is
matched, filed and reported as changed; the to-do waits; Undo restores the state before the match.

### Track E: reading coverage (`reading-coverage`), D49

Goal: the person and Claude know which pages Sprava could not read, and a search never passes off "could not
read" as "is not there". This builds the page and legibility part of D17.

1. Record: the OCR step keeps the recognizer's confidence, which it discards today. Code grades each page good,
   poor or unreadable; a page whose text layer holds almost nothing for its size is graded too. The grade is
   kept with the reading.
2. Show: the filing card says "page 3 of 5 was hard to read" and opens the page. Explore marks the document.
   The binder lists documents not fully read; there the person marks a page as read by them, or types what it
   says, and that text is searchable and marked as theirs.
3. Search: every `search_binder` result carries its coverage: documents searched, and which have pages that
   could not be read. Code repeats it in the turn's summary line.
4. Claude: the session's instructions say to look at a poor page as an image before relying on it, and to say
   so when a claim rests on a page nobody could read.
5. Archives: a folder marked "keep as is" in the binder's settings is never carded, and is searched by file
   name and text.

Tier B where a change touches `Sources/Extract/ExtractHelper.swift`; otherwise tier C.

Done when: an invented five-page scan with a rule on a blurred page 3 is filed and flagged, and a search for
the rule answers that it was not found in readable text and that one document has a page that could not be
read.

## 4. Order and shared files

1. P first: it is short, and the author's own add depends on it.
2. L and D, one worker, D first. Both add to the conversation's tools and its instructions.
3. I beside them, its own worker; it rebases onto L and D for the tool table.
4. M once step 0 has an answer. Steps 1, 4 and 5 do not depend on it and may start earlier.
5. E on the author's go (D49 is recommended, not yet decided).

`Sources/Services/Claude/ClaudeConversationPolicy.swift` gets a sentence from L, D, I and E; whoever merges
second rebases. Merges that change Swift go one at a time, in the order the lead sets.

Two sessions share the work, by the author's instruction of 2026-11-25:

| Session | Builds | Checks |
|---|---|---|
| The lead, on the second Mac | P, D, L, I | M and E: runs each "Done when" on a development build from the other session's branch |
| The session on the author's main laptop | M, E, and the backup fix for a lock left by a killed run | P's fixes, D, L and I, the same way |

The split follows the files: D, L and I share the conversation's tools; M is the IMAP code, and its step 0
needs the mail program on the main laptop; E is the extractor and the index. The session that builds a track
writes its tests and takes it through review. The other session runs its "Done when" on its own Mac, on
invented binders only, before "ready to merge" is sent, and reports what it saw. A finding goes back as orchestra
mail on the track's task, never as a change pushed to the other session's branch.

## 7. Risks

- **A flood of cards** when many files are dropped at once. Track P measures it.
- **A leak through a suggestion.** A script can hold a board id or a name. The card shows the whole text, and
  nothing leaves the Mac before Export.
- **Instructions that only grow.** L's step 5.
- **A rule planted by a document.** Claude may propose what a document told it to. The card shows the rule's
  text and reason, and nothing is saved without the tap.
- **A wrong sent match.** A fallback match is a card, never applied by itself.
