# Plan, 2026-11-13: to daily use, and the to-do list on the phone

Status: working plan, written mid-implementation and updated on 2026-11-14 after its four tracks merged. It
replaces nothing in `docs/mvp.md` or `docs/companion.md`; it says what is unfinished, in what order it gets
done, and who does it. Examples are invented.

## 1. Objective

A Sprava the author uses every day: binders, to-dos, documents, Claude and email on the Mac, and the same
to-do list on a phone through the companion (decisions.md P19). Quality follows the review and test process
v0.3 (`AGENTS.md`): one review round, the full suite once on the final head, small PRs.

## 3. Tracks

Each track is one worker in its own worktree, with one goal. A track is a series of small PRs, each through
the gate, merged by the worker with `scripts/merge-pr.sh`. All four tracks have merged; the text below is kept
as the record of what each one was for, with what changed along the way.

### Track A: pairing on the Mac (`mac-pairing`)

Goal: the person can connect the Mac to a relay and pair or remove a phone from the app.

1. Runtime handlers for pairing and the device list: `companion_pairing_start`, `companion_pairing_poll`,
   `companion_pairing_confirm`, `companion_devices`, on the code the seven PRs landed.
2. Settings › Phone: the relay's address and setup code, claim, the pairing QR code and the six-digit check,
   the list of paired devices.
3. A development companion: a development build keeps its companion credentials in files under the
   development support folder (never the login Keychain), and `sprava dev-phone '<request>'` drives it, so
   the whole flow runs against a local relay without the installed app.
4. Remove, in two PRs. First the key rotation (companion-v0 §4.4): a new epoch, new keys delivered to every
   remaining device, and nothing published at the new epoch until every delivery is uploaded. Then the
   relay's `DELETE` for the device, `companion_device_remove`, `companion_relay_retry` and the Remove button.
   A relay refusal is recorded and shown with Retry; the Mac does not rotate again by itself.

Not built: `companion_reset`. Hiding a binder does not rotate the account key.

Remove is on main (#655), so a paired phone can be taken off again.

Done when: on a development build against a local relay (`companion/relay`, file storage), the web app pairs,
shows up in the device list, and stops working after Remove.

### Track B: publishing and requests (`mac-sync`, then `mac-drain`)

Goal: a shown binder's to-dos reach the phone, and what the phone does comes back.

1. "Show on my phone" in each binder's settings, off by default (companion.md §2, principle 6). A binder's
   identity on the relay is a random 16-byte id kept in Sprava's own state and tied to the binder's place on
   the shelf and to the folder itself (device, inode, creation time); nothing is written into the binder for
   it. A binder that was moved, replaced or restored from backup comes back with the switch off.
2. Start `CompanionJob` in the runtime, with its budget, breaker and Health line (architecture 3.4): publish
   the index and views, publish outcomes. Off until a relay is claimed. Switching a binder off deletes its
   views from the relay, then drops its key.
3. The decision adapter: the drain applies Done, Drop, Follow up, Arrived, Postpone and Note to the binder,
   each with Undo on the Mac.
4. The cards for phone requests the Mac will not apply by itself (companion.md §6): ordinary proposals in the
   binder, shown where other cards are. Done on a repeating to-do is one of them. No change to the binder
   format.
5. The rotation steps in the job, once Track A's rotation is on main: step the rotation until idle, hold
   publication while a delivery is missing, republish every shown binder at the new epoch. A keys upload the
   relay refuses fails the run, which puts it on the Health line; Retry in Settings › Phone clears it.

Later, filed as issues: reconcile devices at the start of a drain (#632); delete superseded relay objects (#633).

Done when: with Track A, a to-do added on the Mac appears in the web app within a publish cycle, Done on the
phone closes it on the Mac with Undo, and a stale Done becomes a card.

3. An end-to-end script: local relay, built web app, a development Mac build; pair, publish, act, read the

## 4. Order and shared files

- The seven companion PRs land first, by the lead. Tracks A and B start from that stack's tip and rebase onto
  main once it has merged; the rebase drops the commits main already has.
- Track A owns `Sources/SpravaApp/Settings` for the Phone screen. Track D does not restructure Settings while
  Track A is open. Track B owns the binder's settings page for its one switch.
- Tracks A and B both add lines to the command table and the runtime's wiring. Whoever merges second rebases.
- Merges that change Swift go one at a time, in an order the lead sets: `merge-pr.sh` refuses when main moved
  during its suite run, and the suite takes about eleven minutes.
- Track C touches `companion/web` and `.github` only.
- Changes under `Sources/sprava-runtime/**` and the companion files on the sensitive-path list are tier B: the
  local review and the cloud review.
